SaaS Employee Attendance, Payroll, Leave & Expense Management System

I want to build a multi-role SaaS application for small and medium-sized businesses/office owners to manage and track employees, working hours, attendance, overtime, leaves, salaries, and business/employee expenses in one centralized system.

The application should be simple, professional, scalable, and easy to use. It should provide different dashboards and permissions for Owner, Accountant, and Supervisor users.

3. Salary Calculation

The application should automatically calculate salaries based on attendance, leave, and overtime.

For example:

An employee has a monthly salary of:

₹30,000

If the company's payroll calculation uses 30 days:

Daily salary = ₹30,000 ÷ 30 = ₹1,000

If the employee is absent for one unpaid day:

Salary deduction = ₹1,000

Therefore:

Final salary = ₹30,000 - ₹1,000 = ₹29,000

The system should allow the Owner to configure the salary calculation basis, such as:

30 days
Calendar days
Working days
Custom company policy

Do not assume that every company uses the same payroll calculation method.

7. Overtime / Extra Hours

Employees may work additional hours beyond their normal working hours to earn extra money.

The application should automatically calculate overtime compensation based on the employee's salary and configured working hours.

For example:

30 working days for calculation
8 normal working hours per day

Daily salary:

₹30,000 ÷ 30 = ₹1,000

Hourly salary:

₹1,000 ÷ 8 = ₹125/hour

If the employee works 2 hours of overtime:

Overtime payment = 2 × ₹125 = ₹250

The system should add the overtime payment to the employee's salary.

25. Employee Salary Advances & Advance Settlement

The application should support employee salary advances.

Sometimes an employee may request and receive an advance amount before the monthly salary is processed. The system should record the advance and automatically account for it during payroll settlement.The advance should be deducted from the employee salary at the  end of the month.Advance should be approved by the owner.

Advance Management

The Owner or an authorized user should be able to record an advance given to an employee.

Each advance record should contain:

Advance ID
Employee
Advance date
Advance amount
Reason
Payment method
Amount already recovered
Remaining advance balance
Expected settlement date
Status
Notes
Created by
Approval status

For example:

An employee has a monthly salary of:

₹30,000

The employee requests an advance of:

₹5,000

The company pays the employee ₹5,000 during the month.

The system should record:

Total Advance = ₹5,000

At the end of the month, when payroll is calculated, the ₹5,000 advance should be deducted from the employee's payable salary.

If there are no other deductions:

Monthly Salary = ₹30,000

Advance Deduction = ₹5,000

Final Salary = ₹25,000

Partial Advance Recovery

The system should also support cases where the entire advance is not deducted at once.

For example:

Employee receives an advance of:

₹10,000

The Owner decides to recover:

₹2,000 per month

The system should track:

Month	Advance Deduction	Remaining Advance
Initial	₹0	₹10,000
Month 1	₹2,000	₹8,000
Month 2	₹2,000	₹6,000
Month 3	₹2,000	₹4,000
Month 4	₹2,000	₹2,000
Month 5	₹2,000	₹0

Once the remaining advance reaches ₹0, the advance should automatically be marked as Settled.

The system should allow the Owner to choose between:

Full deduction in the next payroll
Fixed monthly installment
Custom deduction amount
Manual settlement
Multiple Advances

An employee may receive multiple advances.

For example:

January advance: ₹5,000
February advance: ₹3,000
March advance: ₹2,000

The system should maintain each advance separately while also showing the employee's total outstanding advance balance.

The Owner should be able to see:

Total Advances Given

Total Amount Recovered

Outstanding Advance Balance

Important Business Rule

The system must never deduct more than the outstanding advance balance.

For example:

If an employee has only ₹1,500 remaining on an advance, the system should not deduct ₹2,000. It should deduct only the applicable ₹1,500 and mark the advance as fully settled.

The system should also prevent duplicate deductions for the same payroll period.

All advance creation, modification, approval, recovery, and settlement actions should be recorded in the audit log.

This makes the payroll engine much more complete because the employee's final settlement can now account for attendance + leave + overtime + expenses/allowances + salary advances + other deductions.
